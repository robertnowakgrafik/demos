PERSEIDS

Face north-east and look up.

The meteors can cross any part of the sky, but trace them backwards and they all meet in Perseus, just below the W of Cassiopeia.

The radiant climbs all night. At 22:00 it is 27° above the horizon, by 04:00 it is 67°, which is why the best hour is the last dark one.

PERSEIDS

Perseid Night is a fictional event made for a design demo; the venue, ticket and programme are invented. The astronomy is real and approximate: the radiant position, the parent comet, the entry speed and the rate formula; star positions on the chart are approximate. Everything is drawn in code: meteors in a WebGL fragment shader, the chart on a canvas, and the liquid glass as a lens shader that re-draws the chart inside the card, so the refraction looks the same in Chrome, Firefox and Safari.
